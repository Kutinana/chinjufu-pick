## Visual cross-check (2026-10-07)

Reference: [IJN Destroyers poster](https://naval-encyclopedia.com/POSTERS/IJN-Destroyers-poster.jpg). Compared every mapped class for broad side-profile layout: funnels, principal gun mounts, bridge and mast locations. This is a silhouette check; eras, refits, and proportions are not normalized.

| Class | Comparison result |
| --- | --- |
| Kamikaze | Updated from the supplied illustration; two funnels and principal structures now follow the poster profile. |
| Mutsuki | Two funnels, open gun positions and bridge arrangement broadly agree. |
| Fubuki | Two funnels, three main turrets and bridge arrangement broadly agree. |
| Ayanami | Two funnels, three main turrets and bridge arrangement broadly agree. |
| Akatsuki | Two funnels, narrower forward funnel and three main turrets broadly agree; ONI represents the Hibiki subgroup. |
| Hatsuharu | Two funnels and fore/aft main-gun arrangement broadly agree; the ONI recognition drawing simplifies the bridge and equipment shapes. |
| Shiratsuyu | Two funnels and fore/aft main-gun arrangement broadly agree. |
| Asashio | Two funnels, three main turrets and bridge arrangement broadly agree. |
| Kagerou | Two funnels, three main turrets and bridge arrangement broadly agree. |
| Yuugumo | Compared to the poster’s actual Yugumo row, not the Akigumo row: two funnels and three main turrets broadly agree. |
| Akizuki | One large funnel and four main turrets agree, but ONI draws rounded gun houses whereas the poster depicts more angular ones. Shape conflict is reported; no unrequested turret redesign. |
| Shimakaze | Two funnels, three main turrets and the extended torpedo-mount area broadly agree. |
| Matsu | Two funnels, open forward gun position and aft mounting broadly agree; current icon represents Take in June 1944, poster row represents Matsu. |

The poster places Akigumo at the head of the Yuugumo column. Project ship 132 remains Kagerou class (class 30), ship 19. [Allyn Nevitt’s Kagero Class Notes](https://www.combinedfleet.com/kagero_n.htm) and [Akigumo record](https://www.combinedfleet.com/akigum_t.htm) explain this older classification error and cite Toshio Tamura’s 1994 research. Apart from the Akigumo classification and Akizuki gun-house shape, no obvious class-level structural conflict was identified in this visual pass.
